# Swarm Test Field v2

Fly a field you have never seen from its dock: take off, find the people and the animals on it, report where each one is and which of the two it is, and land back in the dock without ever coming within 1 m of the field's edge. Each seed draws its own field, cut from real ground in Andorra.

This task replaces the first [Swarm Test Field](completed/test_field.md), which flew one fixed field and is completed. It has its own leaderboard, which starts empty. A hotkey submits to one task, ever, so a hotkey that holds a model in the first Swarm Test Field cannot enter this one.

---

A DJI Matrice 4TD sits in its DJI Dock 3 on a field somewhere in Andorra: a meadow by a river, a pasture under a ski slope, a clearing at the edge of a wood, a lawn between the houses of a town. Your model presses take-off, and the dock opens and lifts the drone to 9.5 m. From there your model flies: it looks over the field with a colour camera, a thermal camera and two zoom lenses, and reports each person and each animal it finds, as a person or as an animal, with a box on the image it saw them in and the spot where they stand. Then it presses return home, and the dock flies the drone back and lands it. The field has no fence: its edge is an invisible line, and the drone must stay more than 1 m inside it from take-off to landing. It never flies higher than 10 m above the dock, so the tree crowns that reach over the field are solid obstacles.

**Each seed draws its own field.** Its place, its size, its outline, its trees, the dock's spot, who is on it and where they stand are all drawn from the seed, and a seed is drawn from a key only the validators hold. Nothing about a field can be known before the first observation: a model that works here is one that can search a field it meets for the first time. Some people stand under trees, where a sweep from above does not see them; some seeds are flown at night; the goats, dogs and wild boars that live on the field are to be reported as animals, never as people; and the people and animals outside the edge, the birds and the small animals are there to be left alone.

The drone, the dock, the controls, the cameras, the sensor errors and the link delay are the same as in [Swarm Sentinel](solar_patrol.md). Every field is a real place, and every rule here can be flown on one with the real drone.

Every array arrives as read-only float32: copy it before changing it in place. Each `act()` call gets all five keys, in the same shapes as Sentinel's.

- **`rgb` / `thermal`**: the main camera, colour or thermal, whichever your last action picked. Only the picked feed is drawn; the other one is all zeros. A new frame is taken every 0.5 s, so five decisions in a row see the same image; `frame_age_s` in the state says how old it is.
- **`zoom`**: the last close-up you asked for, all zeros before the first one.
- **`state`**: 31 values, every decision. See [State vector layout](#state-vector-layout).
- **`site_map`**: this seed's field: its edge, the tree crowns that reach it and the houses around it, sent **on the first observation of a patrol only** and all zeros after it. Keep it: it is all your model is told about the place. See [Site map layout](#site-map-layout).

Your program stays alive for the whole patrol and is reset between seeds, so it can keep its own memory.

## State vector layout

The layout is [Sentinel's](solar_patrol.md#state-vector-layout), index for index. Positions count from the dock: the dock is always at 0, 0, 0, wherever the seed stands it. Axes are east, north and up, in metres. Every height in the state is measured **above the dock**; the scoring rules measure height **above the ground straight under the drone**, and the ground differs from seed to seed, so height over the ground is not height over the dock.

Two values mean something of their own here:

| Index | Field | Meaning on the field |
|---|---|---|
| 21 | `flight_limit_distance_m` | Distance to the field's edge, m, never negative |
| 22 | `inside_flight_limit` | 1 inside the field's edge, 0 outside |

`time_left_s` (index 9) counts down from 200. On a night seed the colour feed is dark until night mode is on, as on Sentinel.

## Site map layout

The layout is [Sentinel's](solar_patrol.md#site-map-layout), in metres from this seed's dock.

| Index | Field | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `fence_count` | Number of corners of the field's edge |
| 1–128 | `fence_xy` | The field's edge as (east, north) pairs, in order around it; unused slots are 0 |
| 129 | `limit_count` | The same count |
| 130–257 | `limit_xy` | The same edge: on the field the fence and the flight limit are one line |
| 258 | `table_count` | Number of tree crowns: the field has no panel tables, so these rows carry the crowns |
| 259–578 | `tables` | Up to 64 crowns, nearest the dock first, 5 numbers each: centre east, centre north, the crown circle's diameter, the same diameter again, and 0; unused slots are 0 |
| 579 | `building_count` | Number of houses listed around the field, up to 16 |
| 580–659 | `buildings` | Up to 16 houses, nearest the dock first: centre east, centre north, length, width, heading of the long side in degrees; unused slots are 0 |

The edge is this seed's field: up to 64 corners around 1,378 to 4,133 m², never more than 95 m across. A field with more than 64 tree crowns lists them merged into larger circles, each covering the crowns it stands for; the crown rule is judged on the circles listed. A house row is the smallest rectangle round a building, so the row of an L-shaped house also covers the open ground in its corner.

Everything is in metres east and north of this seed's dock. A report's `report_east` and `report_north` use the same frame.

## Actions

One `act()` returns **26 values**. The first 24 are [Sentinel's](solar_patrol.md#actions), in the same order with the same meaning; two follow them:

| # | Value | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 0–23 | Sentinel's actions | Sticks, gimbal, feeds, zoom, report class, image and box, take-off, return home, cancel. **The report class means something else here:** `report_class` at 0.5 or under names a **person**, over 0.5 an **animal** (in Sentinel, a vehicle) |
| 24 | `report_east` | Where the reported person or animal stands, east of the dock: a share of 100 m, in [-1, 1] |
| 25 | `report_north` | The same, north of the dock |

The two position values are read only when `report` is pressed, together with the class, the image and the box.

**Buttons count once.** `zoom`, `report`, `take_off`, `return_home` and `cancel_return` act only on the decision their value rises through 0.5. Holding one at 1.0 presses it once; to press again, send 0.5 or less for at least one decision first.

Values are clipped to their bounds. An action with any NaN or infinite value, or one that does not hold 26 values, becomes all zeros.

## Cameras and sensors

All of [Sentinel's](solar_patrol.md#cameras-and-sensors): the wide colour camera, the thermal camera, the 3× and 7× zoom lenses, the laser rangefinder, the downward sensors, the wind readings, the position error and the link delay, with the same limits: 80 zooms and 20 reports per patrol. A report that names an animal on a piglet, a bird, a rat or a mouse is neither right nor wrong, but it still uses one of the 20 reports. People are warm in the thermal camera, as Sentinel's are; so are dogs and goats, which a model tells from people by their shape. At night the colour camera is dark unless its night mode is on, and the thermal camera is the one that shows people plainly.

The grass is stood in patches 12 m square, only within 24 m of the field's box, and drawn only where a picture can show it. A colour picture shows a patch when the camera is at most 8 m over the patch's top and at most 24 m across the ground from it, so where the ground rises, grass can show from the take-off height too. A zoom lens magnifies both limits, so it shows the grass in its frame from any height the patrol flies at, but only inside that 24 m border: past it the ground is bare. The thermal camera never shows grass: there the ground is the land's own warmth and shade. Which grass a picture shows follows from its view alone, so the same view always gives the same picture.

## The patrol

One episode is one patrol and one seed, with Sentinel's flight phases: the dock takes the drone up to 9.5 m above it, the model flies, and `return_home` hands it back to the dock to fly home and land. See [Sentinel's patrol](solar_patrol.md#the-patrol) for how take-off, return home, landing and cancel work.

**The 10 m ceiling.** The drone never rises more than 10 m above the dock: like the real dock's height limit, its climb slows and stops there, whether your model, the dock's take-off or its return home is climbing. A climb held at full stick stops within about 0.15 m of the line. The take-off ends 9.5 m above the dock, half a metre under it. Return home follows Sentinel's three cases, except that a return pressed more than 50 m from the dock climbs to 9.5 m, not 20 m; nearer, it keeps its height. The ceiling counts from the dock, as on the real aircraft, not from the ground under the drone: a field can rise and fall up to about 8 m from one end to the other, so over its low end the drone can be more than 10 m above the ground, and over its high end only a few metres above it. There, passing within 5 m of a person, measured in 3D to the top of a person's head, is easy to do: keep your distance across the ground. The flight term's height half, full up to 22 m above the ground, can no longer be lost.

**Tree crowns are solid.** The simulator makes a tree's trunk solid and lets a drone fly through its crown; the real drone would hit it. So while the drone is out of the dock, its position across the ground inside any crown circle ends the patrol as a collision, at any height, and **the seed scores 0**. On an open field a few crowns touch the edge; on a field that reaches into the trees there are a dozen or more, over up to 35 % of it. The site map gives every crown circle (see [Site map layout](#site-map-layout)), and the crowns show in the images.

**The edge rule.** The field has no fence. While the drone is out of the dock (taking off, flying, returning or landing), if its position across the ground is outside the field's edge or 1.0 m or less inside it, the patrol ends on the spot as a `flight_limit` end, **the seed scores 0**, and the trip counts as a mistake under the [×5 rule](#across-the-epoch-the-5-rule). This holds for the dock's own flights too: a return home pressed where the straight line to the dock passes close to the edge ends the patrol on the way. State index 21 gives the distance to the edge every decision.

The patrol ends on the first of:

| End | When | What it keeps |
|---|---|---|
| Landed | The drone settles on the dock's pad after a return | Everything |
| Timeout | 200 s run out | Detection, coverage and height, not the landing |
| Collision | The drone hits anything, the dock included, flies into a tree crown's circle, or tips past its tilt limit | Nothing: the seed scores 0 |
| Flight limit | The drone comes within 1 m of the field's edge, or leaves it | Nothing: the seed scores 0, and the trip is a mistake under the ×5 rule |

The 200 s are planned as 15 s for take-off, 40 s to visit and report the people, 105 s to look over the field, and 40 s to come home and land; only the total is enforced. A return pressed too late ends as a timeout, without the landing.

## The field

Every seed draws its own field, in this order: how many people it holds, a size with room for them, one of the real Andorra areas that holds a field of that size, and an outline cut there.

- **The place.** Real ground, rebuilt from aerial survey: the terrain, its trees, stones, houses and water, with the mountains out to the horizon. The areas are towns, villages, valleys, forests, lakes, rivers, ski slopes, high pasture and scree.
- **The size and shape.** 1,378 to 4,133 m² (half to one and a half times 2,755 m²), in one of four kinds of outline turned any way, at most 95 m across, with at most 8 m between its lowest and highest ground (about the highest and the lowest 1 % of the ground left out) and the plane fitted to the whole field sloping at most 12°, so a small spot can be steeper. No house or water lies inside it.
- **Open or wooded.** Two fields in five lie on open ground, with trees at most along the edge. **Three in five reach into the trees**: four or more trees stand inside the outline and their crowns cover up to 35 % of it.
- **The dock** stands on the field, at least 5 m inside the edge, clear of every tree crown by 3.4 m, on ground no steeper than 10°, where it can fly over everything that stands on the field.
- **Day and night.** Three seeds in five are flown by day, with the sun set by the seed; **two in five at night**, in colder air.
- **Wind** is none, light or strong, drawn per seed in Sentinel's shares for the day or the night.
- **People to find: 0 to 10**, never more than one per 350 m² of field, and nobody in one seed in twenty. They are women and men of twelve builds, from 1.55 to 1.88 m and from slim to heavy, each a few percent taller or shorter than his build, in seven skin tones, with short or long hair, a ponytail, a bun or none, in everyday clothes fit for the seed's air: T-shirts, shirts, sweaters, jackets, trousers, shorts, skirts, sometimes a cap, a beanie, a backpack or a safety vest. Most clothes are plain, some striped, checked or dotted. Everyone in a seed looks different from everyone else in at least two of the top's colour, the head and the colour of the trousers or skirt. Each is at least 2 m inside the edge and 8 m from the dock, and 3 m from everyone who is not with him.
  - **How they hold themselves.** By day 45 % are drawn standing, 30 % low (squatting, kneeling or sitting), and 25 % walking; a walker the field has no room for stands instead. Nobody lies down, and nobody is a statue: someone who stays put keeps busy on his spot (he shifts his weight, talks, looks at a phone, crosses his arms, stretches), and when the flying drone comes within 15 m some look up at it or wave. His place never moves. At night fewer walk (15 %) and more are low (40 %).
  - **Walkers.** Each walker has a patch of his own that he never leaves. About half stroll about one place, in a patch 3 m in radius. The others **walk back and forth across a part of the field**, in a patch 5 to 8 m in radius where the field has the room: up to three stretches in a row without stopping, then a pause. They walk at a walking pace, each in a gait of his own, never a run. When the flying drone comes within 12 m of a walker across the ground he stops: a stroller at the end of the few steps he is on, someone crossing a wide patch within a stride or two, never more than 6 m further on. He looks up at it or waves, and stands about until it has been more than 18 m away for 5 s. So a walker seen from afar may be somewhere else when you come back to him: he is still one person, and a second report on him is a false alarm.
  - **People together.** On the fields as built, about one person in three is **with someone**, and six seeds in ten hold at least one such group. Two to four people who stay put stand together, each 0.9 to 1.9 m from the first of them and never closer than 0.8 m to another, turned to each other and mostly talking. Two walkers may walk as one, a step apart, setting off, turning and stopping together. People who are together are still dressed apart from each other, and each is one person to report, with a box of his own: from some sides one hides part of the other.
  - **Under and beside trees.** On a wooded field, of the people who stay put, about 25 % stand under a tree and 15 % just beside one, at most three under trees in a seed. Someone under a tree is hidden from straight above, at patrol height, and is there only if a drone can see him clearly (about a hundred pixels of him in a wide frame) from at least 1 in 20 of the places it may fly, most of them low and off to the side. Looking under the trees is part of the search.
- **People who are not yours to find.** In a quarter of seeds one to three people walk past **outside** the field, 6 to 40 m beyond its edge. Reporting one is a false alarm.
- **Animals to report.** The **goats, dogs and grown wild boars that live on the field** are yours to report, each as an animal. In under half the seeds there are one or two dogs, or a herd of two to four goats; boars are rarer (see below). Every animal on the ground lives on one side of the edge for the whole patrol: on the field, 3 m or more inside the edge and never under a tree crown, or off it, 6 m or more outside, up to 40 m round the field. One that lives off the field is not yours to report. A field never holds more than 10 people and animals to report together. They are warm in the thermal camera. **Reporting one as a person is a false alarm.**
- **Birds.** In three day seeds in ten, never at night: one to four birds of the place's own kinds (town pigeons, magpies and crows in towns and villages; wood pigeons, magpies and crows in woods and valleys; alpine choughs and crows higher up). A bird stands, looks about, walks, hops and pecks on open ground, and every 15 to 60 s flies 10 to 45 m to another spot, 2 to 5 m over the ground, never over a tree crown or a house. It never reacts to the drone and nothing can collide with it. From patrol height a bird on the ground is a few pixels of a wide frame; in the thermal camera one flying close to the drone can be as large and as square as a crouching person further off, so tell them apart by their outline. It is not asked for: a report that names it an animal counts for nothing, and one that names it a person is a false alarm.
- **Wild boars.** Only where the place is wild enough for them, never in a town or a village, and there in one day seed in twenty and one night in five: one boar, two, or a sow with two to four striped piglets that keep by her. They root across open ground, walk on, look up and sometimes lie down, by night as by day, on the field or up to 40 m round it. A boar is about 1.45 m long and 0.87 m at the shoulder. In the thermal camera at night it is as warm as a person and fills about the area of a crouching one, but it is long and low where a crouching person is square; in the colour camera at night it is hard to see at all. A grown boar that lives on the field is an animal to report; a piglet is not asked for. Reporting either as a person is a false alarm.
- **Rats and mice.** In about one day seed in seventeen and one night in five: one to three rats in a town, a village or by a river, one or two mice anywhere else. Each keeps within about 15 m of where it started: it sniffs about, scurries a few metres, rears up to scent the air and sits up to wash. From patrol height a rat is a few pixels of a wide frame within about 15 m and less than that beyond, and a mouse is one to three pixels; the 7x lens shows them. They are not asked for: a report that names one an animal counts for nothing, and one that names it a person is a false alarm.
- **Never a crowd.** A seed holds at most six animals of every kind together: its goats and dogs, then as many of its birds as fit, then its boars and then its rats or mice, each of those two as a whole group or not at all; where there is no room for them on the ground, a seed that asked for them goes without.

## Scoring

### Per seed

```text
seed = 0.50 · people + 0.20 · animals + 0.20 · coverage + 0.10 · flight
```

The terms are [Sentinel's](solar_patrol.md#per-seed), with the people on the field as the threats:

- **People**, with people on the field: valid reports of people ÷ (people + false alarms). On an empty field it is the coverage ÷ (1 + false alarms).
- **Animals**, with animals to report on the field: valid animal reports ÷ (animals to report + failed animal reports). On a field with none it is the people term ÷ (1 + failed animal reports): such a seed scores as 0.70 · people + 0.20 · coverage + 0.10 · flight as long as no report names an animal wrongly.
- **Coverage**: the share of the field searched, in 2 m cells inside the field's edge and outside every crown circle (no camera above a crown sees the ground under it), seen from 20 m or lower above the ground under the camera, sharp enough that a person would be about 6 pixels across.
- **Flight**: half for landing in the dock after your own `return_home`, half for keeping the peak height above the ground at 22 m or lower, falling to 0 at 30 m; under the 10 m ceiling that half is always earned.

**A seed scores 0** when the drone never takes off, comes closer than 5 m to anyone while out of the dock, trips the edge rule, or collides with anything, a tree crown included. The 5 m are measured in 3D to where the person is at that moment, from the ground under him to the top of his head as he holds himself, and they protect the people passing outside the field too. Animals do not count. Touching the dock's landing pad is not a collision.

**A report is valid** when all of these hold:

1. The frame it names shows something (a colour frame taken in the dark with night mode off does not) and was taken no higher than 24 m above the ground under the camera.
2. The box sits on a body the frame shows: an intersection over union of at least 0.5 with the tight box around its visible pixels. Part of a person seen between leaves is enough, boxed as far as it shows. When a box fits several bodies equally well, with the same overlap on each, the point the report names decides: it is judged on the body of the class it names that stands nearest that point, and a second report that lands on the same body is a repeat. If none of them stands within 1 m of the point, a report that names an animal counts as not asked for when one of those bodies is an animal nobody asks for.
3. That body is a person on the field, the class is person, and it is the first valid report of that person.
4. The reported spot, `report_east` and `report_north` times 100 m from the dock, lies within **1.0 m** across the ground of where the person stood **when that frame was taken**, so a walker is judged where you saw him. Where a person is means the point between the feet of someone standing, crouching or kneeling, and the ground under a walker's hips.

**A report that names an animal** (`report_class` over 0.5) is valid on the same four checks, with the animal in the person's place: the frame, the box on its body, an animal to report (a goat, a dog or a grown boar that lives on the field) reported for the first time, and the spot within 1.0 m of the middle of its body when that frame was taken. One that fails is a **failed animal report**: it lowers the animals term of its own seed and is **never a false alarm**. That covers a box on nothing, an animal that lives off the field (`outside_field`), a second report of the same animal (`repeat`), a spot more than 1 m off (`wrong_position`) and a box on a person (`wrong_class`), who is then still unreported. A report that names an animal on a piglet, a bird, a rat or a mouse is `not_asked`: it counts for nothing, neither for nor against. Naming something an animal when unsure is not free: a person named an animal stays unreported, a missed person, and your model is never told a report's verdict.

A report that names a person and fails is a false alarm, with a verdict that says why: `no_frame`, `too_high`, `no_object`, `animal` (the box is on a dog, a goat, a bird, a boar, a rat or a mouse: an animal named a person), `outside_field` (the box is on someone passing outside the edge), `repeat`, or `wrong_position`, which leaves the person to be reported again. The number of people your model counted is the number of its valid reports: there is no separate count.

Someone seen low and from the side, under a tree, is harder to place within 1 m than someone seen from above: the laser rangefinder, a second look from another side, or a steeper look where the leaves allow it all settle where he stands. The laser reads the grass that the wide colour lens would show from where the drone is, whichever picture was taken last.

### Across the epoch: the ×5 rule

After every seed is scored on its own, **each missed person, each false alarm and each edge trip turns 5 of the model's best seeds to 0** (an animal left unreported and a failed animal report cost only their own seed's animals term, never this), and the score is the mean over all the seeds, the zeroed ones included. It never goes below 0. It is [Sentinel's rule](solar_patrol.md#across-the-epoch-the-5-rule) with one more mistake: a patrol the edge rule ends scores 0 on its own seed and also counts once against the rest (`edge_trips` in the seed's results). A collision, a tree crown included, stays a 0 for its own seed only. A false alarm is a failed report that names a person: a wrong box, a spot more than 1 m off, a person reported twice. A seed your model abandons, by failing on slow-act strikes or by its process exiting, scores 0 and counts every person on the field as missed, the ones it already reported included, from its very first decision.

Over 1,000 seeds, for a model whose seeds average 0.970 and whose best seeds score 1.00:

| Mistakes across the epoch | Seeds set to 0 | Score |
|---|---|---|
| None | 0 | 0.970 |
| One missed person | 5 | 0.965 |
| One missed person and one false alarm | 10 | 0.960 |
| One edge trip | 5, and the tripped seed scores 0 | about 0.964 |
| 200 or more | all 1,000 | 0 |

Each mistake removes five seeds, so a model that never reports, or reports everything, ends at 0 once its mistakes have removed every seed: over 1,000 seeds, 200 of them do it.

## Local testing

Start from the starter agent. It flies a whole patrol from the state and the site map: it takes off, sweeps the field in lanes 9 m apart, at least 7 m inside the edge and 3.5 m off every crown circle, 9 m above the dock, returns home and lands. It never looks at the images and never reports, so it finds nobody, and since it does not know where anyone stands it can pass within 5 m of a person over the field's high end; replace its sweep with your own search. On 12 seeds it landed in the dock every time, after 20 to 126 s, saw 74 to 90 % of the field on 11 of them (on one wooded field its lanes found no way in and it came home after 20 s) and scored about 0.25 a seed, all of it for coverage and flight. Run these from the repo root:

```bash
mkdir -p my_model
cp miner/src/submission_template/test_field_v2_drone_agent.py my_model/drone_agent.py
swarm model test --source ./my_model --family-id cf_test_field_v2
swarm model package --source ./my_model --family-id cf_test_field_v2
swarm model verify --model Submission/submission.zip
swarm benchmark --model Submission/submission.zip --family-id cf_test_field_v2 --seeds-per-group 1 --workers 1
```

- The areas, the people, their motions and every animal ship in swarm-worlds; an install without them refuses the family, naming what is missing. The real areas are fetched beside `swarm-worlds` after the requirements are installed, with `python -m swarm_worlds.areas` (about 2.6 GB to download, 5 GB on disk; `python -m swarm_worlds.areas check` says what is missing). `miner/src/scripts/setup.sh` and the validator image run it; if you installed by hand, run it once.
- Every area brings about 300 MB of its own pictures, and the simulator keeps them until its process ends. A benchmark worker that flies many seeds grows by that much for each new area, so give a long local run memory to spare or fewer seeds a run. A validator's workers and seed checks are restarted before they grow that far.
- Every seed is first flown once by a scripted reference pilot that checks the seed can be done: every person and every animal to report reported, no false alarm and no failed animal report, the edge kept, a landing in the dock, no closer than 5 m to a person, and the dock's own piece of the field's free ground (where the drone can fly) at least half of all of it, so the dock is not cut off from the field. The verdict is cached, so a seed costs this check only the first time.
- To fly one exact seed, pass `--seed-file seeds.json` with `{"schema_version": "challenge_family_seed_file.v1", "family_id": "cf_test_field_v2", "type_seeds": {"type10_testfield": [1002]}}`.
- On a 12-core server the starter's patrol, about 90 s of flight, takes about 65 s to run through the benchmark, and the reference check adds about 80 s of CPU the first time a seed is flown. A worker grows to about 2,950 MiB of memory: keep `--workers` low on a laptop.
- Each seed's line shows its missed people, false alarms and edge trips, and under the table the benchmark applies the ×5 rule to the seeds it flew: the plain mean beside the score after the rule. On a handful of seeds one mistake zeroes most of them; the leaderboard applies the same rule across the epoch's 1,000 seeds.
- Always pass `--family-id cf_test_field_v2`: without it, `swarm benchmark` runs Swarm Sentinel.

To watch a patrol, film it with `swarm video` (it needs a video writer the install does not include), or look around the field with `swarm visualize`:

```bash
uv pip install opencv-python-headless
swarm video --model Submission/submission.zip --seed 1002 --type 10 --family-id cf_test_field_v2 --backend local
swarm visualize --type 10 --family-id cf_test_field_v2 --seed 1002
```
